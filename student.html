01The gap

Two official numbers that can't both be the whole story.

0%

of UAE Grade 12 students pass national literacy benchmarks.

Bayanat open data · Grade 12 pass rates, 2012–2021

vs

0%

of Emirati female students screened at UAE University showed dyslexic features — in Arabic and English.

Aboudan et al., 2011 · IJEL 1(2):64

School exams measure whether you can read. University measures whether you can keep reading 40 dense pages a week, in your second language, at 1am. That is where masked difficulties surface — and where nobody is currently looking.

Our own critique of our own evidence

  • 2007/08 data — almost two decades old
  • One university, female students only
  • Screening features, not clinical diagnoses
  • A pass rate and a trait prevalence aren’t the same metric — that’s the point: K-12 exams measure pass/fail, not reading strain

That isn't a hole in the argument. It is the argument: the most-cited UAE university figure is 17 years old because no one has measured since. Our pilot produces the first current, consented dataset.

02How it works

No quizzes. No labels. Just studying, with someone quietly paying attention.

Smart students mask until university workload catches up. ERLY replaces one-off screening with something that fits real student life — passive, on-device, measured only against your own normal, and finished after four weeks.

    Sign a Support Pact

04The Reading Studio

Every reading, made to fit you.

Open any course PDF in a calm, full-screen studio. Change the font, size and spacing. Have it read aloud in the voice you like. Tap any highlighted word for its meaning in English and Arabic. Noor, the AI reading companion, is there when you want her: talk it through by text or voice, try settings in the Reading Lab, and keep a summary that’s yours. She only checks in if you allow it — and never diagnoses.

  • 5 fonts, size, line, letter and word spacing, line length, 6 page tints
  • Read-aloud with any voice on your device, speed and pitch, word-by-word highlight
  • Noor, the AI reading companion: voice or text chat, plain-word explanations, a Reading Lab and an editable summary — consent first, no labels
  • Glossary with Arabic meanings, line focus, word anchors, a breathing break

    Evaluated and rejected

    • Webcam eye-trackingBiometric class under PDPL, not accurate enough, and confirmation belongs with validated instruments.
    • Keystroke write–delete ratioA privacy violation — and it flags bad typists.
    • Tab-switching countsToo weak to mean anything alone.
    • Quizzes and alerts that imply disabilityNobody takes them, and they stigmatise.

    How ERLY could be misused — and what stops it

    • A parent demands the dataThere is none to hand over: raw logs never leave the device and are deleted on day 28.
    • A university wants to rank studentsSAS sees only what a student shares; cohort views hide any group under 10 students.
    • An employer asks for “ERLY results”ERLY never produces a label or a score — only a private picture the student owns.

    We never collect

    • Anything you type

    What changed, and why

    From always-on to a Support Pact

    Semester-long passive monitoring was our biggest weakness. Now ERLY watches only under a pact you sign, for four weeks, against your own normal — and the Privacy Ledger shows every count it keeps. Raw events are deleted once the summary is written.

    “A false positive costs a workshop invite, not a label. A false negative still has an always-open self-referral door.”

    11Hard questions

    Ask us anything. We already asked ourselves.

    Can ERLY diagnose dyslexia or ADHD?

    No, and it never quotes a % confidence. ERLY triages; it never diagnoses. Sensitivity is ours; specificity belongs to validated screeners and clinicians.

    Do you read my keystrokes?

    Never. DOM event listeners count behaviour — a scroll back, a deleted post, a timestamp — never content.

    Isn’t this just a procrastinator detector?

    Procrastination is executive-function friction, and the help is the same. We treat friction, not labels.

    Why not just ask students what’s wrong?

    Self-reporting is biased and stigmatising, and undiagnosed students often don’t know. Passive telemetry finds the friction; the four-tap Learning Blueprint chooses the support — never the detection.

    What about false positives?

    A false positive costs a workshop invite, not a label. A false negative still has an always-open self-referral door. And an insight needs 3+ domains persisting for two weeks.

    Why a blind fortnight?

    We chose a blind fortnight over a noisy one. Your tools still work from day one.

    Why not eye-tracking?

    Accuracy, biometrics under PDPL, and stigma. Confirmation is a human’s job with validated instruments.

    Won’t university IT block it?

    The Grammarly defence: a student-owned extension on a personal browser needs zero IT procurement for v1.

    Can high-schoolers really deploy on-device models?

    This demo simulates inference on synthetic data and says so. The architecture names real local models and runtimes for the 90-day plan.

    The 17.6% study is old.

    We critique it on stage: 2007/08, one university, women only. That’s the argument — nobody has measured since. Our pilot creates current, consented data.
