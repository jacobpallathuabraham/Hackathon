Quadratic functions and their graphs

Open the lesson · Open its topic page

Topic
Quadratic functions
Course · chapter
Algebra · Quadratics
Level
Secondary / JEE foundation
Time
25 min
Formats
text, visual, video, practice

Learning objectives

  • Recognise a quadratic function and the shape of its graph.
  • Find roots by factorising and read them from a graph.
  • Use the discriminant to count real roots.
  • Find the vertex from the completed-square form.

Before you start

  • Expanding brackets and solving linear equations.

1. The parabola

A quadratic function has the form y = ax² + bx + c, with a ≠ 0. Its graph is a parabola, symmetric about a vertical line through its turning point, the vertex.

  • If a > 0 the parabola opens upwards (a minimum).
  • If a < 0 it opens downwards (a maximum).
  • c is the y-intercept: where the graph crosses the y-axis.

Live model Quadratic equations and the nature of roots — Change a, b and c and watch the vertex, roots and discriminant respond.

  • Try: Make a negative: what happens?
  • Try: Find values where the graph just touches the x-axis.

Assumes: Real coefficients; complex roots are shown when there are no real ones.

Activity

  1. mcq The graph of y = −2x² + 3x + 1 …

    1. opens downwards and crosses the y-axis at 1
    2. opens upwards and crosses the y-axis at 1
    3. opens downwards and crosses the y-axis at −2
    4. opens upwards and crosses the y-axis at 3
    Answer and explanation

    Answer: opens downwards and crosses the y-axis at 1

    a = −2 < 0 → opens downwards; c = 1 → y-intercept 1.

2. Roots

The roots (zeros) are the x-values where y = 0 — where the graph meets the x-axis. If the quadratic factorises, set each factor to zero.

Worked example

Solve x² − 2x − 3 = 0.

  1. Find two numbers that multiply to −3 and add to −2: −3 and +1.
  2. Factorise: (x − 3)(x + 1) = 0.
  3. So x − 3 = 0 or x + 1 = 0.

Answer: x = 3 or x = −1

Activity

  1. expr Expand (x + 4)(x − 2).

    Answer and explanation

    Answer: x^2+2x-8

    (x + 4)(x − 2) = x² − 2x + 4x − 8 = x² + 2x − 8. Any equivalent form is accepted.

Activity

  1. graph The graph shows y = x² − x − 6. What is its positive root?

    Graph: Parabola crossing the x-axis at −2 and 3, with minimum about −6.25 at x = 0.5.

    Answer and explanation

    Answer: 3

    It crosses the x-axis at x = −2 and x = 3. Check: x² − x − 6 = (x − 3)(x + 2).

3. How many roots? The discriminant

The quadratic formula gives the roots of ax² + bx + c = 0:

x = (−b ± sqrt(b^2 − 4ac)) / 2a

The part under the square root, Δ = b² − 4ac, is the discriminant:

  • Δ > 0: two distinct real roots (the graph crosses the x-axis twice);
  • Δ = 0: one repeated root (it touches the axis at the vertex);
  • Δ < 0: no real roots (it misses the axis).

Activity

  1. numeric Find the discriminant of 2x² + 3x + 5.

    Answer and explanation

    Answer: -31

    Δ = 3² − 4 × 2 × 5 = 9 − 40 = −31 < 0, so there are no real roots.

Activity

  1. slider For y = x² + 4x + c, move the slider to the value of c that makes the graph just touch the x-axis.

    Slider c from -2 to 8 (steps of 0.5); Delta = b^2 − 4ac is computed live.

    Answer and explanation

    Answer: c = 4

    Touching means one repeated root: Δ = 16 − 4c = 0, so c = 4. Then y = (x + 2)².

4. The vertex

Completing the square writes the quadratic as y = a(x − h)² + k. The vertex is then (h, k), and the axis of symmetry is x = h = −b / 2a.

Activity

  1. steps Find the vertex of y = x² − 6x + 5.

    1. x-coordinate of the vertex: h = −b / 2a = ?
    2. y-coordinate: substitute x = 3. k = ?
    3. So in completed-square form… (y = (x − 3)² − 4 / y = (x + 3)² − 4 / y = (x − 3)² + 4)
    Answer and explanation

    Answer: step 1: 3; step 2: -4; step 3: y = (x − 3)² − 4

    h = −b/2a, then substitute to find k.

Summary

  • y = ax² + bx + c is a parabola; a > 0 opens up, a < 0 opens down.
  • Roots are where y = 0; factorise or use the formula.
  • Δ = b² − 4ac decides the number of real roots.
  • Vertex at x = −b/2a; completed square y = a(x − h)² + k.

Revision questions

  1. multi Which equations have two distinct real roots?

    1. x² − 5x + 6 = 0
    2. x² + 2x + 1 = 0
    3. x² + x + 1 = 0
    4. 2x² − 3x − 2 = 0
    Answer and explanation

    Answer: x² − 5x + 6 = 0; 2x² − 3x − 2 = 0

    Δ: 25 − 24 = 1 > 0; 4 − 4 = 0 (repeated); 1 − 4 = −3 < 0; 9 + 16 = 25 > 0.

The derivative as a rate of change

Topic
Derivatives
Course · chapter
Calculus · Differentiation
Level
Pre-university / JEE
Time
30 min
Formats
text, visual, practice

Learning objectives

  • Interpret the derivative as the gradient of a tangent and as a rate of change.
  • Differentiate x² from first principles.
  • Use the power rule for polynomials.

Before you start

  • Gradient of a straight line; quadratic graphs.

1. From secant to tangent

The gradient of a curve changes from point to point. To find it at a point P, take a nearby point Q on the curve and draw the straight line through both — a secant. Its gradient is rise over run. As Q slides towards P, the secant turns into the tangent at P, and its gradient approaches the gradient of the curve at P.

Live model Differentiation from first principles — Shrink h and watch the secant become the tangent.

  • Try: Make h smaller and smaller: what number does the gradient approach?
  • Try: Try a negative h.

Assumes: Exact arithmetic for the function shown; the limit is approached numerically.

f'(x) = lim_{h->0} (f(x + h) − f(x)) / h

2. First principles

Differentiate f(x) = x² from first principles

Use the limit definition.

  1. f(x + h) − f(x) = (x + h)² − x² = 2xh + h².
  2. Divide by h: 2x + h.
  3. Let h → 0: the gradient is 2x.

Answer: f'(x) = 2x

Activity

  1. numeric Using f′(x) = 2x, what is the gradient of y = x² at x = 3?

    Answer and explanation

    Answer: 6

    f′(3) = 2 × 3 = 6.

Activity

  1. tf “At the vertex of a parabola, the derivative is zero.”

    Answer and explanation

    Answer: True

    The tangent at a turning point is horizontal, so its gradient is 0.

3. The power rule

Doing the same for xⁿ gives the power rule: multiply by the power, then reduce the power by one. Constants differentiate to 0, and you can differentiate term by term.

d/dx (x^n) = n x^{n−1}

Activity

  1. expr Differentiate y = 4x³ − 5x² + 7x − 2.

    Answer and explanation

    Answer: 12x^2-10x+7

    dy/dx = 12x² − 10x + 7 (the constant −2 differentiates to 0).

Activity

  1. steps A ball’s height is h(t) = 20t − 5t² metres after t seconds.

    1. Find its velocity dh/dt.
    2. At what time is it at its highest point? (velocity = 0)
    3. What is the maximum height?
    Answer and explanation

    Answer: step 1: 20-10t; step 2: 2; step 3: 20

    Velocity is the rate of change of height; the top is where it is zero.

Summary

  • The derivative is the gradient of the tangent, the limit of secant gradients.
  • f′(x) = lim (f(x+h) − f(x))/h as h → 0.
  • Power rule: d/dx xⁿ = n xⁿ⁻¹; constants → 0.
  • Turning points have f′(x) = 0.

Revision questions

  1. mcq If y = x⁵, then dy/dx = …

    1. 5x⁴
    2. x⁴
    3. 5x⁵
    4. 4x⁵
    Answer and explanation

    Answer: 5x⁴

    n xⁿ⁻¹ = 5x⁴.

Flashcards

Power rule
d/dx (xⁿ) = n xⁿ⁻¹

Straight lines: linear functions

Open the lesson · Open its topic page

Topic
Linear functions
Course · chapter
Algebra · Linear functions
Level
Secondary
Time
25 min
Formats
text, visual, practice

Learning objectives

  • Interpret the gradient m as a rate of change (rise ÷ run).
  • Interpret the y-intercept c and match y = mx + c to its graph.
  • Find the equation of a line through two points.
  • Solve a linear equation and read the solution where two lines meet.

Before you start

  • Plotting points (x, y) and substituting numbers into a formula.

1. Gradient: how fast y changes

A linear function has a graph that is a straight line. It can be written y = mx + c.

The gradient m tells you how much y changes when x increases by 1:

  • m = rise ÷ run = (change in y) ÷ (change in x);
  • m > 0: the line goes up from left to right; m < 0: it goes down; m = 0: it is horizontal.

Between two points (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂): m = (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁). Subtract in the same order on top and bottom.

Worked example

Find the gradient of the line through (1, 2) and (3, 8).

  1. Rise: 8 − 2 = 6.
  2. Run: 3 − 1 = 2.
  3. m = rise ÷ run = 6 ÷ 2.

Answer: m = 3: y goes up 3 for every 1 across.

Experience Line builder — Drag m and watch the line tilt: the rise for every 1 step across is always m.

Activity

  1. numeric Find the gradient of the line through (2, 3) and (6, 11).

    Answer and explanation

    Answer: 2

    m = (11 − 3) ÷ (6 − 2) = 8 ÷ 4 = 2.

Activity

  1. mcq For y = −2x + 5, when x increases by 1, y …

    1. decreases by 2
    2. increases by 2
    3. decreases by 5
    4. increases by 5
    Answer and explanation

    Answer: decreases by 2

    m = −2, so each step of +1 in x changes y by −2.

2. The intercept and the whole equation

The y-intercept c is the value of y when x = 0: where the line crosses the y-axis.

So y = 3x − 4 is a line that crosses the y-axis at (0, −4) and rises 3 for every 1 across.

Activity

  1. mcq Where does y = 4x − 3 cross the y-axis?

    1. (0, −3)
    2. (0, 4)
    3. (−3, 0)
    4. (0, 3)
    Answer and explanation

    Answer: (0, −3)

    At x = 0, y = 4 × 0 − 3 = −3, so the line crosses at (0, −3).

Activity

  1. slider The line y = 2x + c must pass through the point (1, 5). Set c.

    Slider c from -5 to 8 (steps of 1); y at x = 1 is computed live.

    Answer and explanation

    Answer: c = 3

    At x = 1, y = 2 + c. For y = 5, c = 3, giving y = 2x + 3.

Activity

  1. graph Which equation matches this line?

    1. y = −x + 2
    2. y = x + 2
    3. y = −2x + 1
    4. y = 2x − 1

    Graph: A straight line falling from left to right, crossing the y-axis at 2 and the x-axis at 2.

    Answer and explanation

    Answer: y = −x + 2

    It crosses the y-axis at 2 (c = 2) and goes down 1 for every 1 across (m = −1).

3. A line through two points

Worked example

Find the equation of the line through (2, 1) and (4, 7).

  1. Gradient: m = (7 − 1) ÷ (4 − 2) = 6 ÷ 2 = 3.
  2. So y = 3x + c. Substitute one point, (2, 1): 1 = 3 × 2 + c.
  3. c = 1 − 6 = −5.
  4. Check with the other point: 3 × 4 − 5 = 7 ✓.

Answer: y = 3x − 5

Activity

  1. steps Find the equation of the line through (1, 4) and (3, 10).

    1. Gradient m = ?
    2. Intercept c = ? (substitute (1, 4) into y = 3x + c)
    3. So the line is … (y = 3x + 1 / y = x + 3 / y = 3x + 4)
    Answer and explanation

    Answer: step 1: 3; step 2: 1; step 3: y = 3x + 1

    m = 3, c = 1: y = 3x + 1. Check: 3 × 3 + 1 = 10 ✓.

Activity

  1. expr Write the equation of the line through (0, 4) and (2, 0). y = ?

    Answer and explanation

    Answer: -2x+4

    c = 4 (it passes through (0, 4)); m = −4 ÷ 2 = −2. So y = −2x + 4. Any equivalent form is accepted.

Activity

  1. numeric A line passes through (−1, 3) and (1, 7). What is its y-intercept c?

    Answer and explanation

    Answer: 5

    m = 4 ÷ 2 = 2; 7 = 2 × 1 + c, so c = 5.

4. Solving: where lines meet

Solving 3x + 4 = 19 asks: for which x does the line y = 3x + 4 reach height 19? Undo each operation in reverse order, doing the same to both sides:

  • subtract 4: 3x = 15;
  • divide by 3: x = 5.

Two lines meet where their y-values are equal, so set the expressions equal and solve.

Activity

  1. numeric Solve 3x + 4 = 19. x = ?

    Answer and explanation

    Answer: 5

    3x = 19 − 4 = 15, so x = 15 ÷ 3 = 5.

Activity

  1. numeric The lines y = 2x + 1 and y = −x + 7 meet at a point. What is its x-coordinate?

    Answer and explanation

    Answer: 2

    2x + 1 = −x + 7 ⇒ 3x = 6 ⇒ x = 2 (and y = 5).

Activity

  1. mcq Solving 5 − 2x = 1, a student writes “−2x = 6”. What went wrong?

    1. 5 was added to 1 instead of subtracted
    2. Nothing — that is correct
    3. They divided by −2 too early
    4. They should have multiplied by 2
    Answer and explanation

    Answer: 5 was added to 1 instead of subtracted

    Subtract 5 from both sides: −2x = 1 − 5 = −4, so x = 2.

Summary

  • y = mx + c: m is the gradient (rise ÷ run), c the y-intercept.
  • m > 0 rises, m < 0 falls, m = 0 is horizontal.
  • Through two points: find m, then substitute a point to find c.
  • Solving an equation = finding where a line reaches a height; two lines meet where their y-values are equal.

Revision questions

  1. tf “A horizontal line has gradient 0.”

    Answer and explanation

    Answer: True

    Moving along a horizontal line, y does not change: rise = 0, so m = 0.

  2. numeric What is the y-intercept of y = 7 − 3x?

    Answer and explanation

    Answer: 7

    At x = 0, y = 7.

  3. numeric Solve 4(x − 2) = 12. x = ?

    Answer and explanation

    Answer: 5

    x − 2 = 3, so x = 5 (or 4x − 8 = 12, 4x = 20).

Flashcards

In y = mx + c, what does m tell you?
The gradient: how much y changes when x increases by 1 (rise ÷ run).
In y = mx + c, what does c tell you?
The y-intercept: the value of y where the line crosses the y-axis (x = 0).
Gradient between (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂)
m = (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁)

The explanation

Vertex form y = a(x − h)² + k shows the turning point (h, k). Expanding gives standard form y = ax² + bx + c. The discriminant b² − 4ac counts the real roots: positive → two, zero → one (the vertex touches the axis), negative → none.     1. misses the axis
    2. touches the axis once
    3. crosses twice
    4. is a straight line
    Answer and explanation

    Answer: touches the axis once

    One repeated root.

  • Vertex form a(x−h)²+k ↔ standard form ax²+bx+c.
  • Discriminant b² − 4ac: >0 two roots, =0 one, <0 none.
  • Factored form a(x−r₁)(x−r₂) shows the roots.

A point travels round a circle of radius 1. Its x-coordinate is cos θ and its y-coordinate is sin θ. Drag the point (or press Play) and watch the two waves being drawn as the angle grows.

Predict first

Between 90° and 180°, sin θ is ___ and cos θ is ___.

  1. positive, positive
  2. positive, negative
  3. negative, positive
  4. negative, negative

The explanation

On the unit circle the point at angle θ is (cos θ, sin θ). As θ goes round, the height traces a sine wave and the horizontal position a cosine wave — the same shape shifted by 90°. In the second quadrant (90°–180°) the point is up and to the left: sin positive, cos negative. tan θ = sin θ / cos θ is undefined where cos θ = 0 (90°, 270°). Because x² + y² = 1, sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 always.

Apply it

  1. mcq cos 180° equals…

    1. 1
    2. 0
    3. −1
    4. undefined
    Answer and explanation

    Answer: −1

    The point is at (−1, 0).

  2. mcq For every angle, sin²θ + cos²θ equals…

    1. 0
    2. 1
    3. 2
    4. it varies
    Answer and explanation

    Answer: 1

    Pythagoras on the unit circle.

Summary

  • Point on the unit circle = (cos θ, sin θ).
  • Signs by quadrant: ASTC.
  • sin²θ + cos²θ = 1.

The explanation

Each transformation sends a point (x, y) somewhere new. Reflection in the x-axis: (x, −y); in the y-axis: (−x, y). The explanation

Surface area is the total area of the faces — exactly the area of the net. Volume measures space inside. Scaling every length by k multiplies areas by k² and volumes by k³: double a cube’s edge and its surface area becomes 4× larger but its volume 8×. What a, b and c do to a parabola

Transcript (from the captions file)

This is the simplest quadratic: y equals x squared. Its graph is a parabola: a symmetric curve with one turning point, called the vertex. The number in front of x squared is a. Make a bigger, and the parabola gets narrower. Make a smaller, and it opens out wider. And if a is negative, the parabola flips upside down: it now has a maximum instead of a minimum. The constant term, c, slides the whole curve up and down. It is also where the graph crosses the y-axis. The roots are where the curve meets the x-axis. For x squared minus two x minus three, they are minus one and three. The discriminant, b squared minus four a c, tells you how many roots there are before you draw anything. Positive: two roots. Zero: the curve just touches the axis. Negative: it misses the axis completely.
