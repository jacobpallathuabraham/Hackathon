Number words (Spanish & French)

Open the live experience

Kind
Construction studio (interactive)
Time
about 8 min
The question it starts from
Watch any number up to 9 999 999 assemble itself in Spanish and French — from veintiún mil to quatre-vingt-dix-neuf.
What the model simplifies
French in the 1990 recommended spelling (hyphens throughout).

Introduction

Numbers are built from a few parts and some rules. Choose Spanish or French, set any number up to 9 999 999, and watch it assemble — every part is coloured by the rule that made it. Then test yourself.

Predict first

In French, 80 is “quatre-vingts”. That literally means…

  1. eight tens
  2. four twenties
  3. eighty
  4. four and twenty

What to do

  • Spanish: compare 16–19, 21–29 and 31. Why are some written as one word and some with “y”?
  • Spanish: 100 is “cien” but 101 is “ciento uno”. Try 21 000 and 1 000 000.
  • French: count 69, 70, 71, 79, 80, 81, 90, 99. Which numbers are counted in twenties?
  • French: when does “cent” or “vingt” take an s?

Controls

Language

  • Español — button
  • Français — button
  • Number — slider (0 to 1000, step 1)
  • Number to write in words — number (0 to 9999999)
  • Test me — button

The explanation

Spanish: 0–29 are single words (16–29 joined: dieciséis, veintidós); from 31, tens and units are joined by y (treinta y uno). 100 alone is cien, otherwise ciento; 500 is the irregular quinientos. Before mil and millones, uno shortens to un (veintiún mil). French counts in tens up to 69, then in twenties: 70 = soixante-dix (60 + 10), 80 = quatre-vingts (4 × 20), 90 = quatre-vingt-dix. et joins the 1s up to 71 (vingt-et-un, soixante-et-onze), but not 81 or 91. vingt and cent take an s only when multiplied and at the very end (quatre-vingts, deux-cents, but quatre-vingt-un, deux-cent-un), and mille never does. ERLY writes French with the 1990 recommended spelling: hyphens between every part.

Apply it

  1. mcq Spanish for 21 000 is…

    1. veintiuno mil
    2. veintiún mil
    3. veinte y uno mil
    4. veintiuno miles
    Answer and explanation

    Answer: veintiún mil

    uno shortens to un before mil: veintiún mil.

  2. mcq French for 71 is…

    1. septante-et-un
    2. soixante-et-onze
    3. soixante-onze
    4. quatre-vingt-onze
    Answer and explanation

    Answer: soixante-et-onze

    60 + 11 joined with et: soixante-et-onze.

  3. mcq Which is spelt correctly?

    1. quatre-vingts-un
    2. quatre-vingt-un
    3. quatre-vingt-et-un
    4. quatre-vingts-et-un
    Answer and explanation

    Answer: quatre-vingt-un

    No s when another number follows, and no et for 81.

Summary

  • Spanish: 0–29 one word; from 31 tens “y” units; cien/ciento; uno → un before mil/millones.
  • French: twenties from 70; et only for 21–71; -s on vingt/cent only when multiplied and final.

Telling the time

Open the live experience

Kind
Experiment (interactive)
Time
about 6 min
The question it starts from
Drag the clock hands and hear where Spanish and French switch from “past” to “to” the hour.
What the model simplifies
Spoken 12-hour conventions.

Introduction

Drag the clock hands — or use the sliders — and read the time aloud in Spanish and French. Watch where each language switches from counting past the hour to counting to the next one.

Predict first

In Spanish, 2:45 is usually said as…

  1. son las dos y cuarenta y cinco
  2. son las tres menos cuarto
  3. son las dos menos cuarto
  4. es la tres y cuarto

What to do

  • Move from 1:00 to 2:00. Why “es la una” but “son las dos”?
  • Find the minute where both languages switch to counting down to the next hour.
  • French: compare 12:30 and 3:30 — demi or demie?

Controls

  • Hour (24-hour) — slider (0 to 23, step 1)
  • Minute — slider (0 to 59, step 1)

Above, each control is listed with the label the student sees. Moving a control changes the live model and its readout. Parts of the picture can also be dragged.

The explanation

Both languages tell the time past the hour up to half past and to the next hour after it. Spanish uses y (past) and menos (to), cuarto for a quarter and media for half; one o’clock is singular (es la una), the others plural (son las dos). French puts heure(s) after the number, says et quart, et demie and moins le quart, and uses midi (noon) and minuit (midnight); after midi/minuit it is et demi, because they are masculine. Timetables in both languages often use the 24-hour clock instead (son las catorce cuarenta y cinco; il est quatorze heures quarante-cinq).

Apply it

  1. mcq “Es la una y media” is…

    1. 12:30
    2. 1:30
    3. 1:15
    4. 2:30
    Answer and explanation

    Answer: 1:30

    una = 1, y media = half past.

  2. mcq In French, 4:45 is “Il est … moins le quart”.

    1. quatre heures
    2. cinq heures
    3. trois heures
    4. six heures
    Answer and explanation

    Answer: cinq heures

    A quarter to five.

Summary

  • Past the hour up to :30, then to the next hour.
  • Spanish: y / menos, cuarto, media; es la una, son las dos.
  • French: et quart, et demie, moins le quart; midi, minuit.

Introduction

Regular verbs follow patterns. Choose a language and a verb; ERLY splits it into stem + ending for every person, so you can see the pattern — then test yourself.

French “parler” (to speak) with “nous” becomes…

Compare an -er and an -ir verb in French. In Spanish, compare hablar, comer and vivir: which endings are shared? Then try the quiz.

The explanation

For a regular verb, remove the infinitive ending (-er, -ir, -re; -ar, -er, -ir) to find the stem, then add the ending for each person. French -er: je parle, nous parlons, vous parlez, ils parlent (the -e, -es, -ent endings are silent). Spanish -ar: hablo, hablas, habla, hablamos, habláis, hablan. Irregular verbs (être, ser, ir…) do not follow these patterns and must be learned separately.

Apply it

  1. mcq Spanish “vivir” with “nosotros” is…

    1. vivemos
    2. vivimos
    3. vivamos
    4. vivís
    Answer and explanation

    Answer: vivimos

    -ir verbs: -imos.

  2. mcq French “finir” with “ils” is…

    1. ils finent
    2. ils finissent
    3. ils finons
    4. ils finis
    Answer and explanation

    Answer: ils finissent

    -ir verbs add -issent.

Summary

  • Stem + ending for each person.
  • French -er/-ir/-re; Spanish -ar/-er/-ir patterns.
  • Irregular verbs must be learned separately.

Ordering at a French café

Open the live experience

Kind
Conversation (interactive)
Time
about 6 min
The question it starts from
Order a drink in French without freezing.
What the model simplifies
One polite, standard café exchange; real conversations vary.

Introduction

You are at a café counter in Paris. The server speaks first. Choose what to say — some answers are polite and natural, some are understood but blunt, and one is a classic mistake. Listen plays the French with your device’s voice (only when you press it).

Predict first

How do you politely ask for something in a French café?

  1. « Je voudrais… » (I would like…)
  2. « Je suis… » (I am…)
  3. « J’ai… » (I have…)
  4. « Donnez-moi… » (Give me…)

What to do

Work through the conversation. Try a wrong answer on purpose to see what it actually means.

Controls

  • Listen: Bonjour ! Vous désirez ? — button

Your reply

  • Je voudrais un café, s’il vous plaît.I would like a coffee, please. — button
  • Un café.A coffee. — button
  • Je suis un café.I am a coffee. — button

Every experience also has the step buttons Hook · Predict · Explore · Observe · Explain · Apply · Connect, and Pause and Reset for the model. Above, each control is listed with the label the student sees. Moving a control changes the live model and its readout.

The explanation

Je voudrais (“I would like”) is the polite standard for ordering; add s’il vous plaît. Je suis means “I am”, so « Je suis un café » means “I am a coffee”. Short answers like « Sur place » or « Non, merci » are perfectly natural replies.

Apply it

  1. mcq « Sur place ou à emporter ? » means…

    1. Eat in or take away?
    2. Is that all?
    3. How much is it?
    4. Hot or cold?
    Answer and explanation

    Answer: Eat in or take away?

    « Sur place » = here (eat in); « à emporter » = to take away.

  2. mcq The server says « Ça fait quatre euros cinquante ». How much do you pay?

    1. €4.50
    2. €14.50
    3. €5.40
    4. €40.50
    Answer and explanation

    Answer: €4.50

    quatre = 4, cinquante = 50: €4.50.

Summary

  • Je voudrais… , s’il vous plaît: I would like…, please.
  • Sur place / à emporter: eat in / take away.
  • Ça fait…: that comes to…
  • Je suis = I am (not “I would like”).
