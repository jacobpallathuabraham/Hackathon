01The gap

Two official numbers that can't both be the whole story.

0%

of UAE Grade 12 students pass national literacy benchmarks.

Bayanat open data · Grade 12 pass rates, 2012–2021

vs

0%

of Emirati female students screened at UAE University showed dyslexic features — in Arabic and English.

Aboudan et al., 2011 · IJEL 1(2):64

School exams measure whether you can read. University measures whether you can keep reading 40 dense pages a week, in your second language, at 1am. That is where masked difficulties surface — and where nobody is currently looking.

Our own critique of our own evidence

  • 2007/08 data — almost two decades old
  • One university, female students only
  • Screening features, not clinical diagnoses

That isn't a hole in the argument. It is the argument: the most-cited UAE university figure is 17 years old because no one has measured since. Our pilot produces the first current, consented dataset.

02How it works

A clear start, a clear end, and nothing in between you didn't agree to.

The first version of ERLY watched all semester. We cut that. Now ERLY tests first, watches for two weeks only if you say yes, and then switches itself off.

04Your private result

Two possible answers. Neither of them is a label.

Finish all five days (or load a sample above) and your result appears here. The thresholds in this demo are placeholders; the pilot sets real cut-offs by comparing scores with professional assessments.

05The 2-week watch · opt-in

A contract with a start date, an end date and a stop button.

Games show how you do on a good five minutes. Real coursework shows how you do on week 9. The watch checks whether game results show up in real study — and then it ends.

06Your insight

A picture, not a label.

After day 14 the games and your two weeks come together in a page made for you. It uses drawings, weather and plain words to show what might be getting in the way and what helps.

07The hand-off

From “worth a closer look” to a real appointment — with someone beside you.

The test and the two weeks build evidence strong enough to justify a referral. Only a professional assessment can confirm a learning disability. SAS does that part.

A

Your Evidence Pack

Game scores, the Arabic–English comparison, questionnaire totals and the 2-week summary. You download it; you decide who sees it.

B

Bring an ally

Invite someone you trust. They get a short Support Playbook — what to say, what to avoid — never your scores.

Try saying

“Thanks for telling me. Want me to come with you?”

Please avoid

“You just need to try harder.”

C

Book SAS

One tap. Your intake is pre-filled from the Evidence Pack, your ally can attend, and you get a check-in 48 hours later.

Don't want to play the games? You don't have to.

“Where do your cut-offs come from?”

We don't invent them. Games compare you with peers, so the pilot first collects same-language, same-year scores and sets cut-offs against professional assessments. Demo thresholds are labelled as placeholders.

Practice effects & anxiety

One retry per day is allowed, and the better score counts. Timers are visible but never flash red, and no one else ever sees your scores.

Masking

Bright students can pass five-minute games and still struggle under real workload. That's what the 2-week watch is for — and why self-referral is always open.

Triage, not diagnosis

The test and two weeks justify a referral. SAS confirms with validated instruments. Sensitivity is ours; specificity belongs to clinicians.

10Privacy by design

The best privacy feature is a finish line.

We never collect

  • Anything you type
  • Camera or webcam — ever
  • Voice recordings (naming is processed on-device)
  • Emotion or facial analysis
  • Location
  • Anything after day 14
What changed, and why

From always-on to two weeks

Semester-long passive monitoring was our biggest weakness. Now monitoring only exists after a test result, only with consent, and only for 14 days. Raw events are deleted once the summary is written.

“A false positive costs a workshop invite, not a label. A false negative still has an always-open self-referral door.”

Aligned with

  • PDPL — Federal Decree-Law 45/2021Consent: specific, time-limited, revocable.
  • UAE Charter for the Development & Use of AI (2024)A human confirms every outcome.
  • National Policy for Empowering People of Determination (2017)Early identification and inclusion.
  • UAE AI Strategy 2031AI for education and wellbeing.

13Hard questions

Ask us anything. We already asked ourselves.

Can five games diagnose dyslexia?

No, and we never say they do. The challenge and the two weeks build evidence strong enough to justify a referral. A professional assessment at SAS confirms.

Where do your cut-offs come from?

Demo thresholds are placeholders and labelled as such. The pilot collects same-language, same-year peer scores and sets cut-offs against professional assessments.

Nobody takes screening quizzes. Why would they play this?

It's not a screening quiz — it's “How do you learn best?”, run for the whole cohort in orientation week, with a useful profile for every player and a streak for finishing.

Isn't monitoring creepy?

That's why we cut it to two weeks, only after a result, only with consent, with a visible countdown and a stop button. Raw data is deleted after the summary.

What about bright students who pass but still struggle?

Masking is real. The self-referral door is always open, with or without a test.

Won't people get better the second time?

Yes — practice effects. One retry is allowed, the better score counts, and the pilot builds its norms the same way.

Why both Arabic and English?

If reading is slow only in English, it may be a language barrier. If it's slow in both, it's more likely a reading difficulty. The comparison makes the bilingual blind spot visible.

Why not leaderboards?

Ranking people on these tasks would be harmful. Streaks reward finishing; scores are never shown to anyone else.
