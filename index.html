01The gap

Two official numbers that can't both be the whole story.

0%

of UAE Grade 12 students pass national literacy benchmarks.

Bayanat open data · Grade 12 pass rates, 2012–2021

vs

0%

of Emirati female students screened at UAE University showed dyslexic features — in Arabic and English.

Aboudan et al., 2011 · IJEL 1(2):64

School exams measure whether you can read. University measures whether you can keep reading 40 dense pages a week, in your second language, at 1am. That is where masked difficulties surface — and where nobody is currently looking.

Our own critique of our own evidence

  • 2007/08 data — almost two decades old
  • One university, female students only
  • Screening features, not clinical diagnoses

That isn't a hole in the argument. It is the argument: the most-cited UAE university figure is 17 years old because no one has measured since. Our pilot produces the first current, consented dataset.

02Why university, why now

Three realities that keep students invisible.

i.

Masking runs out of road

Bright students compensate for years — extra hours, memorisation, a parent who reads aloud. Then the university workload triples and the strategy that worked since Grade 3 quietly collapses.

ii.

The bilingual blind spot

When an Emirati student struggles in English-medium courses, everyone — including the student — calls it a "language barrier". Stigma does the rest. Nobody asks whether Arabic reading is hard too.

iii.

Strain that looks the same

Burnout, anxiety and trauma produce the same re-reading, freezing and missed deadlines as a learning disability. A good early system has to route both — to different doors.

03How it works

One semester, eight steps — and the student holds the key to every door.

  1. 01

    Opt in, for the tools

    Students install ERLY because it's useful on day one: read-aloud, dyslexia-friendly fonts, line focus, focus timers. Signal tracking is a separate, granular switch.

  2. 02

    The Learning Blueprint

    Four rapid-fire questions about strengths, not weaknesses. They decide which tools ERLY offers later — auditory learners get audio, visual thinkers get mind-maps.

  3. 03

    Seal a Support Pact

    Advance consent: who may be involved if a pattern persists. Revocable any time, expires every semester, and re-confirmed at the moment it would trigger.

  4. 04

    A quiet baseline window

    Weeks 1–6 ERLY only learns what normal looks like for you. No insights, ever, in this window. We chose a blind start over a noisy one.

  5. 05

    Convergence, then confirmation

    An insight is only considered when 3+ signal dimensions stay above your own baseline for 2+ weeks. Then an optional, on-device Reading Rhythm Check confirms it.

  6. 06

    An evidence card, to you first

    Bilingual, plain-language, listing exactly which three signals contributed. No diagnosis, no percentage, no label. Just "this has been harder — here's what might help."

  7. 07

    Ally Bridge & warm handoff

    The student may invite their chosen ally, who gets a Support Playbook — what to say, what not to say. One tap books a joint SAS session with a pre-filled intake.

  8. 08

    SAS closes the loop

    The student exports an Evidence Pack that turns a guess-heavy first appointment into a focused one. Consented, anonymised outcomes recalibrate the model.

      07What makes it different

      Built for the student first, useful to everyone around them.

      Flagship

      Ally Bridge + Support Playbook

      Disclosure led by the student. The person they chose receives a short, kind playbook — what to say, what not to say, how to suggest SAS without making it sound like a verdict.

      Say

      “Thanks for telling me. Want me to come with you?”

      Avoid

      “You just need to try harder.”

      Flagship

      Self-baselining

      You vs. you — never vs. the class average. A naturally slow reader isn't flagged for being slow; only for changing.

      Flagship

      On-device edge AI

      ERLY looks at your textbook, never at you. Signals, baselines and cards are computed in the browser extension.

      Flagship

      Evidence Pack

      A signal timeline plus which tools changed the pattern. Clinicians start with a pattern, not a hunch.

      Friction Fingerprint

      Where strain happens: English vs. Arabic, dense PDFs vs. slides, time of day, distance to deadline. "Your re-reading spikes in English PDFs after 11pm."

      Curriculum Mirror

      When 40% of a class hits reading friction on the same Week 4 PDF, the problem isn't 40% of brains — it's the PDF. Professors get an anonymous nudge, with a visual summary and audio version ready to share.

      Syllabus Sync & Exam Mode

      Midterms raise everyone's load. ERLY knows the calendar, relaxes its expectations, and swaps flags for focus sprints.

      Warm Handoff

      Pre-filled intake, the ally can attend, a 48-hour follow-up, and an accommodation request drafted for the student to send.

      SAS Bridge Letter

      After a confirmed assessment, a formal accommodation request tailored to the student's friction profile — in Arabic or English.

      Feasibility

      The Grammarly Defence

      A student-owned extension on a personal browser needs zero university IT procurement for v1. Universities join for the SAS dashboard — not as a gatekeeper to the student's tools.

      08The AI, honestly

      Four layers. None of them needs a dataset of labelled disabled students — the data that shouldn't exist publicly.

      L1

      Self-baseline anomaly detection

      Unsupervised. Robust z-scores (median / MAD) per signal against the student's own baseline window, smoothed weekly, then the 3-signal × 2-week convergence rule.

      in demo
      L2

      Explainable insight cards

      On-device bilingual templates. Every card names its three contributing signals and its Friction Fingerprint. No cloud LLM ever touches personal data in v1.

      in demo
      L3

      Outcome calibration

      Semi-supervised. Consented, anonymised outcomes from SAS assessments tune signal weights and thresholds — federated, so raw data still never moves.

      roadmap
      L4

      Contextual reinforcement via micro-nudges

      Single-tap "did it help?" questions inside the flow feed a Thompson-sampling bandit, filtered by the Learning Blueprint. Preferences improve without survey fatigue.

      Why not eye-tracking?

      We evaluated webcam eye-tracking and rejected it: it's a biometric class of data under PDPL, laptop webcams aren't accurate enough to mean anything, and confirmation belongs with validated instruments in a clinic. It stays on the clinical-side roadmap only.

      12Hard questions

      Ask us anything. We already asked ourselves.

      Are you logging my keystrokes?

      No. ERLY listens to DOM events — a scroll, a "delete post" button, a timestamp. It measures behaviour, never content. It can't read what you type because it never asks the browser for it.

      Isn't this just catching procrastinators?

      Procrastination is executive-function friction, and the intervention is the same: micro-tasks, focus sprints, a workshop. We treat friction, not labels.

      Why not just ask students what their problems are?

      Self-reporting is biased and stigmatising, and undiagnosed students often don't know there's anything to report. Passive signals find the friction; the Learning Blueprint finds the solution.

      What if university IT blocks it?

      The Grammarly Defence: it's a student-owned extension on a personal browser. No procurement for v1.

      What about false positives?

      A false positive costs a workshop invite, not a label — and the student sees the card first and can dismiss it. A false negative still has an always-open self-referral door.

      What's your confidence percentage?

      We never quote one. We use persistence and convergence. Specificity is the clinic's job, with validated instruments.

      Your main study is from 2011.

      Yes — and we critique it on stage. That it's still the reference figure proves nobody has measured since. The pilot produces current data.

      Why are the first weeks blind?

      A baseline needs time. We chose a blind start over a noisy one — and the tools are useful from day one, so students still get value.

      What about students who are already diagnosed?

      Utility mode: read-aloud, summaries, timers, the letter drafter — with all signal tracking switched off.

      Why not use eye-tracking?

      Accuracy, biometrics and stigma. Webcam gaze is noisy, it's biometric data under PDPL, and confirmation should be a human with a validated instrument.

      Can high-schoolers really ship quantised on-device models?

      In the hackathon we simulate inference and say so. The architecture names real models and runtimes — WebLLM, Web Speech, Piper — so the 90-day plan is buildable, not hand-waved.

      Is this buildable in 48 hours?

      We're demoing the logic and the UX on synthetic data, not a live scraper. The detection engine on this page is real code; the data feeding it is labelled synthetic.
